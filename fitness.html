Beginner guide · Core & body composition

The Core
Circuit

A 15-minute, 3x/week routine — plus the calorie, strength and sleep math that actually determines whether it shows.

Fat loss doesn't happen locally. When you exercise, your body releases fatty acids from stores all over — not just the area you're working. A randomised 12-week trial found no extra belly-fat reduction in people who added abdominal training to a diet change compared to diet alone. A 2013 study had participants do high-rep leg presses with only one leg; the trained leg didn't lose more fat than the untrained one.

So: core exercises build the muscle underneath. Overall calorie balance, strength training, and sleep determine whether you can see it. You need both — here's how to do both.

The core circuit

3x per week, ~15 min. Do these one after the other as a circuit, 45 seconds rest at the end, then repeat for 3 rounds.

1

Dead bug

8 reps / side

The single best starting point — it teaches you to brace without wrecking your lower back.

  • Lie on your back, arms straight up toward the ceiling, knees bent 90° over your hips (tabletop)
  • Press your lower back flat into the floor — imagine flattening a sheet of paper under your spine. Hold this the entire time
  • Slowly lower your right arm overhead and extend your left leg out, stopping a few inches off the floor
  • Pause, return to start, switch sides
Cue: slow and controlled, no momentum. The moment your lower back arches off the floor, you've gone too far — shorten the range.
NASM — How to do a Dead Bug
2

Front plank

30–45 sec
  • Forearms on the floor, elbows directly under your shoulders, feet hip-width
  • Straight line from head to heels — no sagging hips, no piked butt
  • Squeeze your glutes and tuck your pelvis slightly under
  • Gaze slightly in front of your hands, not down at your feet
Cue: the glute squeeze + pelvic tuck is what turns a plank from a shoulder hold into a core exercise. Stop when your form breaks, not when the timer ends — a clean 30 seconds beats a sagging 90.

Modification: drop to your knees.

3

Reverse crunch

10–12 reps

Targets the lower abdominal region, which is harder to reach with standard crunches.

  • Lie flat, knees bent, thighs perpendicular to the floor, shins parallel to it
  • Hands flat on the floor beside you for stability
  • Curl your pelvis toward your chest, lifting your hips off the floor — a small movement, maybe 3–4 inches
  • Pause at the top, then lower slowly with control
Avoid: swinging your legs and using momentum to throw your hips up. If you can't lift your hips without a swing, you're not ready for the range yet — just do the pelvic tilt portion.
Well+Good — How to do a Reverse Crunch
4

Side plank

20–30 sec / side
  • Lie on your side, elbow under your shoulder, feet stacked or staggered
  • Lift your hips so you form a straight line from head to feet
  • Don't let your top hip roll forward or your bottom hip sag

Modification: bottom knee bent on the floor, lifting from the knee instead of the feet.

5

Glute bridge

12–15 reps

Not an ab exercise, but it balances the front-loaded work and fixes the anterior pelvic tilt that makes a stomach look less flat even at the same body fat.

  • On your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width, heels close to your glutes
  • Drive through your heels, squeeze your glutes, lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders
  • Don't arch your lower back at the top — the movement comes from the glutes, not the spine
  • Hold 2 seconds at the top, lower slowly

The part that actually flattens it

Core work alone won't do it. Structure a week like this:

DayFocus
MonFull-body strength + core circuit
Tue30–45 min brisk walk or easy cardio
WedRest or walk
ThuFull-body strength + core circuit
FriCardio (intervals if you like them)
SatFull-body strength + core circuit
SunRest

The three levers that matter more than any ab exercise:

  • 1
    A modest calorie deficit — roughly 300–500 below maintenance. Aggressive deficits cost you muscle, which is the thing giving you shape.
  • 2
    Resistance training for your whole body — squats, hinges, presses, rows, carries. This determines the ratio of fat to muscle in the weight you lose, which is what changes how you look.
  • 3
    Sleep and stress — 7–9 hours. Elevated cortisol and disrupted hunger hormones from poor sleep work directly against you, and this is the lever most people with demanding jobs are quietly failing.
    Protein at roughly 0.7–1g per pound of bodyweight makes the deficit far easier to sustain and protects muscle.

Expect this to take months, not weeks. Where you lose fat first is largely down to genetics and sex — men typically lose from the trunk earlier than women, and the abdomen is often the last place it comes off regardless.

One caveat: if you've got any history of lower back issues or a recent abdominal surgery, run the plank and crunch work past a physio first — dead bugs and bird dogs are the safe substitutions in the meantime.
